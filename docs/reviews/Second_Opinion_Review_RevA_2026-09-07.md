# Second-Opinion Review — ESP32-S3 Protected Field I/O Controller, Rev A

*A second, independent review of the plan v7.3 §4 specification was obtained on 2026-09-07 (fifteen numbered points and a verdict). This file weighs each point against the design document as it stood after the first specification review, with arithmetic where the two disagree, and records what was adopted. Method: same as the first review — datasheets and [`../calcs/board2_calcs.py`](../calcs/board2_calcs.py); sections 5, 7, 10 and 12b of the script were extended for this pass.*

**Outcome: 9 points adopted (three of them already in the design and made explicit), 6 not adopted with reasons.** The second review independently reached the same four headline conclusions as the first — retire the 9 V claim, resize the PPTC, freeze a real buck part, and treat the USB/24 VAC ground path as a hazard — which is worth noting: two reviews from different directions agreeing on the blocking items is evidence the findings are real.

## 1. Point-by-point

| # | The second review said | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Publish **12–36 V DC / 18–30 VAC**, not 9–36 V DC. | **Adopted for the lower bound; 30 VAC rejected.** | 12 V and 24 V are the two systems the board is for, so 12 V is the published minimum; the arithmetic (first review, finding 2) still guarantees 10 V at full load, and the design document keeps that as the engineering floor. **30 VAC is not adopted**: 30 × √2 = 42.4 V peak → 41 V bus, 2 V under the SMBJ43A's 43 V standoff and above the 39 V the whole front end is sized to. The ceiling stays **28 VAC**. |
| 2 | 1.1 A / 60 V PPTC, ≥ 2 A bridge, SMBJ43A, **470 µF / 100 V** bulk because the TVS can clamp at 69 V. | **Already in the design except the 100 V; 100 V not adopted.** | The first three were the first review's findings 3 and 16. The 100 V argument confuses the TVS's clamp *rating* with what the node *does*: the electrolytic is in parallel with the TVS, so the bus can only reach 69 V if the capacitor is charged to 69 V. The worst thing on a 24 VAC secondary — a solenoid valve's 22 mJ turn-off kick (0.5 H, 0.3 A) — raises 470 µF from 34 V to **35.3 V**; a 600 W-class surge of 8.6 A for 50 µs raises it 1.3 V. The node cannot reach the clamp voltage with the capacitor present, and if the capacitor fails open, the 85 V buck survives on the TVS alone. 63 V at a 39 V bus (62 % of rating) stands; it is also the common Ø 12.5 mm footprint. |
| 3 | Freeze a real part; prefer **LM5012** (100 V, 2.5 A) to remove the 60 V-abs-max-vs-clamp problem. | **"Freeze a real part" — done (LMR38020). LM5012 not adopted.** | The problem the LM5012 solves is already solved: the LMR38020's 85 V absolute maximum passes the SMBJ43A's 69.4 V clamp at full surge (first review, finding 4). What the LM5012 costs is exactly what a first buck should avoid: constant-on-time control needs a **ripple-injection network** (R–C–C on the feedback) designed to a formula, plus an **external catch diode** (their SS510) with its loss and its own layout loop, plus a switching frequency that moves with input voltage. The LMR38020 is synchronous, fixed-frequency, internally compensated, has a spread-spectrum option, a TI 5 V design example with exact values, and 1.7 k in stock at LCSC on the day. The second review's own UVLO (9.0 / 8.4 V) is consistent with its 12 V minimum, as this design's 7.0 / 6.1 V is with its 10 V floor. |
| 4 | LM5012 values: 300 kHz, 33 µH, SS510, 158 k / 49.9 k, RON 41.2 k, UVLO 9.0 / 8.4 V. | **Not applicable** (part not adopted). | The numbers are internally consistent for an LM5012 (1.2 V reference: 1.2 × (1 + 158/49.9) = 5.0 V; 33 µH at 300 kHz gives ≈ 0.45 A of ripple at a 39 V bus). Recorded here in case Rev B ever wants a 100 V stage. |
| 5 | Replace the AP2112K with **AP7361C-33E-13** (1 A, SOT-223). | **Adopted.** | The first review flagged the LDO as a watch item; the second review is right that "watch it" is the wrong answer for a board that never sleeps in a furnace closet. Same 0.19 W sustained / 0.49 W peak: AP2112K in SOT-23-5 (≈ 150 °C/W on a pour) = 29 / 73 °C rise; AP7361C in SOT-223 (110 °C/W datasheet, ≈ 70 °C/W with the tab on a pour) = 14–21 / 34–54 °C. Verified against the AP7361C datasheet (DS37274): 2.2–6.0 V in, 1 A, 360 mV dropout at 1 A, 60 µA IQ, ≥ 1 µF in / ≥ 2.2 µF out ceramic, 150 °C shutdown; 3-pin SOT-223 has no EN. LCSC **C500795**. C502 becomes 4.7 µF. The AP2112K stays as the BOM alternate. |
| 6 | Replace PC817 + 1N4148 with **LTV-814** AC-input optocouplers; channel = 2 × 4.7 kΩ + LTV-814 + **47 kΩ pull-up + 1 µF**. | **The 47 kΩ + 1 µF filter adopted; the LTV-814 and the 9.4 kΩ not adopted.** | The filter: 47 kΩ × 1 µF is the same τ = 47 ms as the first review's 10 kΩ × 4.7 µF, but the opto now has to sink 70 µA instead of 330 µA — 9× margin at the 12 V / derated-CTR corner instead of 2×, headroom for the LED's CTR to halve over its life, a plain 0603 capacitor with no bias derating, and a 4 ms first-detect. Strictly better; adopted. The part: an LTV-814's *minimum* CTR is 20 %, against the EL817 rank C's 200 %; and its built-in anti-parallel LED does not remove D701, because the series indicator LED (first review, finding 9) needs the reverse path anyway. The 9.4 kΩ series value would leave the indicator LED at 0.95 mA at 12 V — too dim; 4.8 kΩ (two 2.4 kΩ 1206) stays. Side effect accepted and documented: the input threshold drops from ≈ 7.6 V to ≈ 3.5–4 V (the two LED drops plus a little), still 3× below a 12 V signal and above a power-stealing thermostat's 1–2 V of leakage. |
| 7 | Keep **FIELD_COM off logic ground**; no "field pour joined at a star point"; relay contacts a third floating domain; describe the board honestly. | **Already the design; wording made explicit.** | The design never grounded FLD_COM — the "star point" in the plan and in the design document is the *rectifier return*, which is logic ground by the nature of a non-isolated buck. The design document's isolation map already said which boundaries are isolated; a sentence naming the three domains (logic, input, relay contacts) was added to §2 so nobody can misread it the way the second review read the plan. |
| 8 | Make field power and USB **mutually exclusive**; put **DISCONNECT FIELD POWER BEFORE USB** on the silk; OTA in the field; isolated USB as Rev B. | **Adopted.** | The first review found the same hazard (finding 7) and wrote a bench rule; the four-word silk rule is the better universal statement, and it now stands beside J401. The bench exception is kept and spelled out: the plug-in 24 VAC transformer and an unearthed bench supply are floating, and a battery-powered laptop has no earth path — bring-up steps 9–10 rely on that. OTA was already in the firmware contract. |
| 9 | Relay: S8050 + 680 Ω is sensible; use **SS14** for the coil flyback; keep **100 kΩ** base pull-down; snubber/MOV footprints DNP; publish ≤ 2 A / ≤ 30 V. | **Rating and snubbers already the design; SS14 optional; 100 kΩ rejected.** | The 1N4148W is rated 150 mA continuous against a 79 mA coil and the flyback pulse lasts a millisecond — adequate; an SS14 would work equally (slightly slower release from its lower Vf) and is a free substitution if the SOD-123 footprint is ever awkward. **100 kΩ is not adopted**: against a pin with a default pull-up (~45 kΩ) a 680 Ω + 100 kΩ base network sits at 2.3 V — the relay pulls in at reset. 10 kΩ holds it at 0.6 V and is used on every output (first review, finding 5). |
| 10 | The condensate use case is **fail-operational, not fail-safe**; the board is supervisory and must not replace the OEM float-switch interlock. | **Adopted.** | Correct and important for anything a client reads. README, design document (§1, §14, §17) and firmware contract now say "supervisor, in series with the OEM interlock, never instead of it", and the NC choice is stated as an availability trade-off. |
| 11 | MOSFET: keep 100 Ω / SS14; **100 kΩ** gate pull-down; **JP901 open by default**; connector **VLOAD+ · GND · OUT1− · OUT2−**; 400 mA allocation. | **Jumper default and connector order adopted; 100 kΩ rejected.** | Open-by-default: with the jumper closed, an external 8–12 V supply on VLOAD would be forced onto the buck output and the two 5 V relay coils — destructive. Open by default makes that impossible until someone closes it deliberately; the pump demo closes it once. The connector order puts the supply pair on adjacent screws and marks the switched pins as returns. 100 kΩ on the gate against a pull-up pin = 2.3 V > the AO3400A's 0.65 V threshold = ON at reset; 10 kΩ stays. |
| 12 | Add a **field-power sense divider**: 1 MΩ + 1 MΩ / 82 kΩ (1:25.4), 10 nF, stays < 2.75 V at the 69 V clamp. | **Already in the design; ratio raised for the reason given.** | VIN_SENSE existed (first review, finding 11) at 100 k / 6.8 k, which would have put **4.4 V** on the pin at a 69 V clamp — over the ESP32-S3's 3.6 V absolute maximum. The second review's point is right; its 2 MΩ divider is more source impedance than an ESP32 ADC likes even with a capacitor. Chosen: **2 × 100 kΩ / 10 kΩ (1:21)** — 3.29 V at 69 V, 1.86 V at the 39 V design bus, 15 mV of bus per LSB, 186 µA, and the 100 nF makes a 20 ms low-pass. |
| 13 | Budget ≈ 0.93 A; design for **1.1 A continuous**, acceptance-test at **≈ 1.25 A**. | **Adopted.** | The two budgets agree (0.93 vs 0.95 A). Testing the rail at 1.25 A — a 4 Ω / 10 W resistor — instead of 0.8 A is the right acceptance discipline; at 12 V in that is 0.72 A through the PPTC, under the 1.1 A hold. Success criterion 1 and bring-up step 7 changed. |
| 14 | Keep Board 1's ESP32-S3 RF and boot architecture; antenna clear of the buck. | **Already the design.** | §7 and the hard rules (10, 12–14). |
| 15 | First powered test at **≈ 250 mA**, not 100 mA at 12 V. | **Adopted, with a split.** | A Wi-Fi transmit peak needs 0.36 A × 5 V ÷ 0.9 ÷ 9.9 V ≈ **200 mA** at the 12 V input, so a 100 mA limit would fake a brownout on a programmed board. 100 mA is kept for the very first power-up of the unprogrammed board (the ROM bootloader idles at a few tens of mA and the point is to catch a short); the limit rises to 250–300 mA before any firmware that uses the radio. |

## 2. What changed in the design document because of this review

U501 → AP7361C-33E-13 (SOT-223) with C502 = 4.7 µF · opto filter → 47 kΩ + 1 µF · VIN_SENSE → 2 × 100 kΩ / 10 kΩ · JP901 open by default · J901 = VLOAD+ · GND · OUT1− · OUT2− · silk **DISCONNECT FIELD POWER BEFORE USB** · published input 12–36 V DC (10 V guaranteed floor) · rail acceptance at 1.25 A · bring-up current limit split 100 / 250–300 mA · condensate use case reworded as supervisory · three-domain isolation sentence. Every change is also in Addendum A of the design document, the pin map, the bring-up guide, the BOM tables and the LCSC cart draft.

## 3. Where the two reviews disagree, for the record

| Topic | First review / design | Second review | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buck | LMR38020, 80 V, synchronous, internally compensated | LM5012, 100 V, COT with ripple injection, external diode | LMR38020 — less design risk, the abs-max check already passes |
| Bulk capacitor | 470 µF / 63 V | 470 µF / 100 V | 63 V — the node cannot reach the clamp voltage with the capacitor present |
| Optocoupler | EL817 rank C + 1N4148W | LTV-814 | EL817 — 10× the minimum CTR; the diode is needed either way |
| Pull-downs | 10 kΩ | 100 kΩ | 10 kΩ — reset-state pull-ups |
| AC input ceiling | 28 VAC | 30 VAC | 28 VAC — 41 V bus vs 43 V standoff |
| Input threshold | ≈ 7.6 V (10 k) → now ≈ 4 V (47 k) | — | accepted the lower threshold for the sink margin |
