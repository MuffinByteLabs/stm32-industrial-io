# Specification Review — ESP32-S3 Protected Field I/O Controller, Rev A

*Review of the frozen Board 2 specification (PCB Freelance Plan v7.3, §4) before schematic capture. Date 2026-09-07. Method: every number in §4 was re-derived independently from component datasheets (LMR38020 SNVSC40E, SRD-05VDC-SL-C, EL817/PC817, AO3400A, KBP2xx, 1N4148W, SMBJ series, Bourns SRR1260, Littelfuse/Bourns 60 V PPTC families) and from the ESP32-S3 datasheet, then checked against the plan's own claims. Arithmetic lives in [`../calcs/board2_calcs.py`](../calcs/board2_calcs.py) — run it, the numbers below come from it. Scope: electrical specification only; layout does not exist yet.*

**Outcome: 4 blocking findings, 6 risky, 12 improvements, and 11 plan numbers confirmed as written.** Everything is resolved in the design document ([`../ESP32S3_FieldIO_Final_Design_Document.md`](../ESP32S3_FieldIO_Final_Design_Document.md)); this file is the record of *why* the design document differs from plan §4.

> **Superseded details (same day, by the [second-opinion review](Second_Opinion_Review_RevA_2026-09-07.md)) — the findings stand, three of the fix values moved:** finding 1's fix is now **47 kΩ + 1 µF** (same τ = 47 ms as 10 kΩ + 4.7 µF, five times the sink margin); finding 11's divider is now **2 × 100 kΩ / 10 kΩ (1:21)** so a 69 V clamp stays under the ESP32's 3.6 V pin maximum; the published input is **12–36 V DC** with the 10 V figure kept as the guaranteed floor (decision 2 below). The table is left as written — it is the record of what this review found.

Severity scale (same as Board 1's reviews): **BLOCKING** = the board would not do what the spec says · **RISKY** = works, with a real failure mode waiting · **IMPROVE** = works, better with a small change · **CONFIRMED** = the plan's number is right as written.

## 1. Findings table

| # | Severity | Where (plan §) | Finding | Evidence | Fix applied in the design document |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **BLOCKING** | 4.5 opto inputs | **10 kΩ + 1 µF (τ = 10 ms) does not hold a steady LOW through the 60 Hz zero-crossing gap.** With the anti-parallel diode the opto conducts for only ≈ 7 ms of each 16.7 ms cycle at 24 VAC; during the ≈ 9.5 ms gap the 1 µF charges through 10 kΩ to **≈ 2.0 V** (2.3 V with cap derating) — above the ESP32-S3's VIL of 0.825 V, into the undefined band. The AC thermostat use case (#1, "safest") would read as chatter. | `board2_calcs.py` §7: V = 3.3·(1 − e^(−9.5/10)) = 2.03 V | **C = 4.7 µF X7R 16 V 0805** (τ = 47 ms → node stays ≤ 0.74 V worst case), release time ≈ 140 ms, plus a firmware rule of 3 consecutive 10 ms samples. Design doc §8. |
| 2 | **BLOCKING** | 4.3 + 4.4 | **UVLO ≈ 7.5 V contradicts the 9 V DC input claim.** At 9 V in, the bus after PPTC and bridge is 6.9 V (full load) to 7.6 V (light load). A 7.5 V turn-on never starts at 9 V in half the tolerance band, and a 6.6 V turn-off shuts the buck down the moment the load arrives — precisely the chatter the UVLO exists to prevent. | `board2_calcs.py` §1, §3 | UVLO **7.0 V turn-on / 6.1 V turn-off** (typ; RENT 91 k / RENB 20 k). Input spec restated as **10–36 V DC guaranteed** (worst-case EN tolerance), ≈ 8.5 V typical; 24 VAC unchanged. Design doc §4. |
| 3 | **BLOCKING** | 4.3 power entry | **0.5 A-hold PPTC trips at the low end of the DC range.** Full 5 V load is 4.76 W; at 9 V in the input current is 0.79 A, at 12 V it is 0.54 A — both above a 0.5 A hold current, before temperature derating. Also: the obvious 1.1 A radial part, Bourns MF-R110, is **30 V-rated** — a trap. | `board2_calcs.py` §1; LCSC C208481 lists MF-R110 at 30 V | **1.1 A hold, 60 V radial PPTC** — Littelfuse 60R110 or Bourns **MF-RX110** (the RX suffix is the 60 V family). Hand-soldered, sits next to the terminal. Design doc §3. |
| 4 | **BLOCKING** | 4.4 buck part | **LMR36015 is a 2 × 3 mm leadless VQFN-HR** — the joints cannot be inspected, which defeats the board's own DIY rule ("hand-inspectable, no BGA"). The 100 V fallback named in the plan, LM5164, is a **1 A** device: 5 % margin over the 0.95 A worst case, and TPS54160 needs an external compensation network on a first buck. | TI datasheets; `board2_calcs.py` §1 | **LMR38020SDDAR** (TI): 4.2–80 V, 2 A, synchronous, internal compensation, **HSOIC-8 PowerPAD (leaded, 1.27 mm pitch)**, 400 kHz via RT, LCSC **C3192337** (1.7 k in stock, $1.32). Its 85 V absolute maximum also passes the plan's own TVS test (SMBJ43A clamps at 69.4 V) without leaning on the bulk cap. Alternate in the BOM: LMR16020PDDAR (C190006, 60 V, 2 A, non-synchronous — needs an SS36 catch diode, an RT resistor and a 0.75 V FB divider). Design doc §4. |
| 5 | RISKY | 4.7 MOSFET outputs | **100 kΩ gate pull-down cannot guarantee OFF at reset.** ESP32-S3 pins with a default internal pull-up (~45 kΩ) would sit at 3.3 × 100/145 = **2.3 V** — above the AO3400A's 0.65 V minimum threshold. The datasheet lists GPIO0, 1, 2, 3, 43, 44 (and the flash pins) as pulled up at reset. | ESP32-S3 datasheet Table 2-1 "At Reset"; AO3400A VGS(th) 0.65–1.45 V | **10 kΩ pull-downs on every gate and base**, and outputs assigned only to pins with no default pull (IO9–IO13). 10 k gives 0.60 V even against a hypothetical pull-up. Design doc §7, §10. |
| 6 | RISKY | 4.2 "USB never powers field loads" | **With one OR'd 5 V node the relays *would* click on USB power.** USB gives ≈ 4.6 V after the Schottky; the SRD-05VDC must-operate voltage is 3.75 V. The README claim "outputs stay off until field power is present" would be a firmware promise, not a hardware fact. | SRD-05VDC-SL-C datasheet (pick-up ≤ 75 %); SS14 Vf | **Split the rail**: relay coils and VLOAD hang on **5V_BUCK** (buck output, exists only with field power); logic runs on **5V_SYS** = OR(5V_BUCK → SS14, USB → SS14). Now physics enforces the README claim, USB can never feed a pump, the buck can never back-feed a laptop, and the AP2112K dissipates 25 % less (4.6 V in, not 5.0 V). Design doc §2, §4. |
| 7 | RISKY | 4.3 bridge + 4.2 USB | **Ground loop through the USB cable when the field supply is earthed.** A bridge puts one diode between the field return and board GND. If the field supply's negative (or the 24 VAC "C" wire) is bonded to earth and the laptop is earthed through its PSU, the USB ground bypasses that diode and the board's return current flows through the USB cable. | Topology; standard bridge-rectifier caveat | Kept the bridge (polarity-agnostic input is the product story and the demo transformer is a floating 2-pin plug-in unit) and made the rule explicit everywhere: **USB only on the bench, with a floating supply or a battery-powered laptop; never USB while field-powered from an earthed supply; OTA is the field update path.** Listed in README safety notes, BringUp §0, and design doc §17. |
| 8 | RISKY | 4.5 opto series resistors | **Two 2.4 kΩ 0805s are at their 125 mW rating at 36 V DC** (113 mW each) — and 36 V is a voltage this board's own power terminal hands out. At 30 V they run at 76 mW (61 %). | `board2_calcs.py` §7 | **Two 2.4 kΩ 1206** (250 mW each, 200 V rated): 45 % of rating at 36 V. Same placement effort. Design doc §8. |
| 9 | RISKY | 4.5 indicator LEDs "logic side" | **A logic-side LED loads the opto's output.** An LED + resistor on the collector node needs another ≈ 1.3 mA of sink; at 12 V in, the opto has only ≈ 0.65 mA to give (CTR derated), so the channel could light the LED and still fail to reach logic LOW. | `board2_calcs.py` §7 | **Indicator LED in series with the opto LED, on the field side**, inside the same anti-parallel diode. It lights with the real field current, independent of firmware, costs no GPIO and no opto sink current; the input threshold rises to a useful ≈ 7.6 V. Design doc §8. (Alternative kept in the doc: mirror in firmware on 4 spare GPIOs.) |
| 10 | RISKY | 4.7 VLOAD external | **An external VLOAD supply wired backwards shorts through the MOSFET body diodes and the SS14 flybacks.** Nothing in the plan protects it. | Topology | Accepted risk, documented: large **VLOAD +** silk, README rule, and "meter the pump supply before it meets J901". A series Schottky would cost 0.4 V at 1 A on a 5 V pump rail — not worth it. Design doc §10, §17. |

## 3. Decisions closed by this review

1. **Buck: 60 V vs 100 V** — closed by choosing the 80 V LMR38020: TVS clamp (69.4 V) < absolute maximum (85 V) at full surge, so the check passes on its own. (With any 60 V-class part the check fails above ≈ 7 A of TVS current and only the 470 µF bulk cap — which absorbs 8.6 A for 50 µs with a 1.3 V rise — saves it. That argument is in the design doc as a footnote, not as the reason.)
2. **Input range** — 10–36 V DC guaranteed / ≈ 8.5 V typical, 24 VAC (18–28 VAC). "9–36 V DC" is retired from every client-facing line.
3. **Rail topology** — 5V_BUCK (field-powered loads) and 5V_SYS (logic, OR'd from buck and USB) are separate nets.
4. **Input indicator LEDs** — field side, in series with the opto LED.
5. **PPTC** — 1.1 A / 60 V radial.
6. **Parts and time estimates** — restated (finding 14).
